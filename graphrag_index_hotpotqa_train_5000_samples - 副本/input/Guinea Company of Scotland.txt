Guinea Company of Scotland

The Guinea Company of Scotland was a short-lived Scottish trading company, active during the 1630s.

 It was granted a royal monopoly over the trade with West Africa by Charles I, modelled on the existing English Guinea Company, with which it unofficially co-operated.

 The company made only a single voyage, of two ships; one returned, whilst the other was seized by Portuguese forces at São Tomé and its crew killed.

 Following this, the company made some attempts to recover compensation for the second ship, but without any success, and ceased to operate sometime around 1639.

 There was no further attempt by Scotland to trade with Africa on an organised basis until the formation of the Company of Scotland Trading to Africa and the Indies in 1695.

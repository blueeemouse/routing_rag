The Presbyterian Guardian

On 7 October 1935, Rev. John Gresham Machen established "The Presbyterian Guardian" to serve as the voice of conservative Presbyterianism in the United States, the main denomination of which, the Presbyterian Church in the United States of America, had become fairly liberal and modernist.

 James T. Dennison, Jr., compiler of a cumulative index to the magazine, wrote that "The Presbyterian Guardian" was "conceived it controversy."

 Machen had been encountering significant theological disagreements with Dr. Samuel G. Craig, editor of "Christianity Today" (1930–1949) (not to be confused with "Christianity Today", the magazine founded in 1956).

 In response to perceived modernism in the magazine, Machen began publishing The Guardian with a small group of conservative ministers and laymen, hiring Rev. H. McAllister Griffiths as the first Editor.

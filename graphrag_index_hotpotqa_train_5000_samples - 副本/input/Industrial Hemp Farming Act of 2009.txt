Industrial Hemp Farming Act of 2009

The Industrial Hemp Farming Act of 2009 is a bill that was introduced in the United States House of Representatives by Ron Paul (R-Texas) and Barney Frank (D-Massachusetts) on April 2, 2009.

 Also known as H.R.1866, the bill clarifies the differences between marijuana and industrial hemp as well as repeals federal laws that prohibit American farmers from cultivating industrial hemp.

 Industrial hemp is the non-psychoactive, low-THC, oilseed and fibers varieties of the cannabis sativa plant.

 Hemp is a sustainable resource that can be used to create thousands of different products including fuel, fabrics, paper, household products, and food and has been used for hundreds of centuries by civilizations around the world.

 If H.R.1866 passes American farmers will be permitted to compete in global hemp markets.

 On March 10, 2009, both Paul and Frank wrote a letter to their Congressional colleagues urging them to support the legislation.

 This bill was previously introduced in 2005 under the title of Industrial Hemp Farming Act of 2005.

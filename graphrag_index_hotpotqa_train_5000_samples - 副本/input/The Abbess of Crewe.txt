The Abbess of Crewe

The Abbess of Crewe is a novel published in 1974 by Muriel Spark.

 It is centred on a Catholic convent in Crewe and the political intrigues surrounding the election of a new abbess, after the death of the former.

 It exhibits Spark's typical style of crossing seamlessly between temporal points in the narrative.

 Michael Lindsay-Hogg adapted the novel into his film "Nasty Habits", released in 1977.

 This book is considered an allegorical treatment of the Watergate scandal.

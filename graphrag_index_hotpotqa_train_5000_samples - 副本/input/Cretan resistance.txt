Cretan resistance

The Cretan resistance (Greek: "Κρητική Αντίσταση") was a resistance movement against the occupying forces of Nazi Germany and Italy by the residents of the Greek island of Crete during World War II.

 Part of the larger Greek Resistance, it lasted from 20 May 1941, when the German "Wehrmacht" invaded the island in the Battle of Crete, until the autumn of 1945 when they surrendered to the British.

 For the first time during World War II, attacking German forces faced in Crete a substantial resistance from the local population.

 Cretan civilians picked off paratroopers or attacked them with knives, axes, scythes or even bare hands.

 As a result, many casualties were inflicted upon the invading German paratroopers during the battle.

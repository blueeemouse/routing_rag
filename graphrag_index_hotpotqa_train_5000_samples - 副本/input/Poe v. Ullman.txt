Poe v. Ullman

Poe v. Ullman, 367 U.S. 497 (1961) , was a United States Supreme Court case that held that plaintiffs lacked standing to challenge a Connecticut law that banned the use of contraceptives, and banned doctors from advising their use, because the law had never been enforced.

 Therefore, any challenge to the law was deemed unripe, because there was no actual threat of injury to anyone who disobeyed the law.

 The same statute would later be challenged yet again (successfully) in "Griswold v. Connecticut" (1965).

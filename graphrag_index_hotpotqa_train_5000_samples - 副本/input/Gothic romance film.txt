Gothic romance film

The Gothic romance film is a Gothic film with feminine appeal.

 Diane Waldman wrote in "Cinema Journal" that Gothic films in general "permitted the articulation of feminine fear, anger, and distrust of the patriarchal order" and that such films during World War II and afterward "place an unusual emphasis on the affirmation of feminine perception, interpretation, and lived experience".

 Between 1940 and 1948, the Gothic romance film was prevalent in Hollywood, being produced by well-known directors and actors.

 The best-known films of the era were "Rebecca" (1940), "Suspicion" (1941), and "Gaslight" (1944).

 Less well-known films were "Undercurrent" (1946) and "Sleep, My Love" (1948).

 Waldman describes these films' Gothic rubric: "A young inexperienced woman meets a handsome older man to whom she is alternately attracted and repelled."

 Other films from the decade include "The Enchanted Cottage" (1945) and "The Heiress" (1949).

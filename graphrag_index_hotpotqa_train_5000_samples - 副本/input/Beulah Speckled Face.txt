Beulah Speckled Face

The Beulah Speckled Face is a breed of domestic sheep originating in the United Kingdom.

 Having been bred in the uplands of Wales for more than a hundred years, a breed society was officially formed in 1958.

 This breed is most common in Eppynt, Llanafan Fawr, Abergwesyn, and Llanwrtyd Wells, and it is little known outside Wales.

 The origins of the breed are unclear; it may be a truly native breed that has been selected to suit the local environment for centuries.

 The breed is named for its distinctly patterned black and white face, which is free from wool.

 The ewes, which are naturally polled, are often crossed with lowland rams such as the Suffolk, Texel or Bluefaced Leicester to breed mules, and to produce market lambs for meat.

 When bred pure, the lambs do not meet today's export demand for lean, fast-growing sheep.

 However, the ewes make good mothers and produce plenty of milk.

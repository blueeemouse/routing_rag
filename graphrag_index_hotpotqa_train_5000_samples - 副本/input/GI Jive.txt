GI Jive

GI Jive was a 15-minute radio program transmitted by the Armed Forces Radio Service for entertainment of soldiers in World War II.

 Its initial frequency of five days per week later increased to six days per week.

 It was included in the group of "programs proposed for production on the AFRS's initial schedule".

 "GI Jive" differed from AFRS programs like "Command Performance" and "Mail Call" in that it used a disc jockey format, with someone playing popular recordings of the day.

 In contrast, "Command Performance" and "Mail Call" transcribed live performances by popular entertainers.

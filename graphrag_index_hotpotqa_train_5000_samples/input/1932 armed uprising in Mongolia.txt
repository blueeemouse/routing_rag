1932 armed uprising in Mongolia

The 1932 armed uprising (Mongolian: 1932 оны зэвсэгт бослого , "1932 ony zevsegt boslogo") was a revolt against the rule of the Mongolian People's Revolutionary Party (MPRP) and its "left course" directed by Soviet Bolsheviks and Komintern in the Mongolian People's Republic.

 It covered the northwest part of the country and lasted from April–November 1932.

 The principal leaders were lamas. Main part of ordinary rebels consisted of common people but even many party members and the local bureaucracy joined the rebellion.

 The insurgents were inspired by rumours of support from the Panchen Lama and the Japanese.

 They were noted for a number of atrocities they committed, but the Mongolian troops sent with Soviet support to quell the rebellion engaged in more brutalities.

 More than 1500 people were killed.

 Special study revealed that this uprising corresponds to generally accepted criteria of the civil war.

 Suggestions that the uprising was inspired or supported by Japan or the 9th Panchen Lama were not confirmed by archival documents.

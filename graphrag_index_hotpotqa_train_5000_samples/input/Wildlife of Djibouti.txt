Wildlife of Djibouti

The Wildlife of Djibouti, consisting of flora and fauna, is in a harsh landscape with forest accounting for less than one percent of the total area of the country.

 The flora and fauna species are most found in the northern part of the country in the ecosystem of the Day Forest National Park at an average altitude 1500 m , including the massif Goda, with a peak of 1783 m .

 It covers an area of 3.5 km2 of "Juniperus procera" forest, with many of the trees rising to 20 m height.

 This forest area is the main habitat of critically endangered and endemic Djibouti francolin, and another recently noted vertebrate, "Platyceps afarensis".

 The area also contains many species of woody and herbaceous plants, including boxwood and olive trees, which account for sixty percent of the total identified species in the country.

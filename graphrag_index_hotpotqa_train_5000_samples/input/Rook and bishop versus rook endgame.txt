Rook and bishop versus rook endgame

The rook and bishop versus rook endgame is a chess endgame where one player has just a rook, bishop and king, and the other player has only a rook and king.

 It has been studied many times through the years.

 This combination of material is one of the most common pawnless chess endgames.

 It is generally a theoretical draw, but the rook and bishop have good winning chances in practice because the defense is difficult.

 Ulf Andersson won the position twice within a year, once against a grandmaster and once against a candidate grandmaster; and grandmaster Keith Arkell has won it 18 times out of 18 .

 In positions that have a forced win, up to 59 moves are required .

 Tony Kosten has seen the endgame many times in master games, with the stronger side almost always winning .

 Pal Benko called this the "headache ending" .

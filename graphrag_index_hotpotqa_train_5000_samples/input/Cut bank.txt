Cut bank

A cut bank, also known as a river cliff or river-cut cliff, is the outside bank of a water channel (stream), which is continually undergoing erosion.

 Cut banks are found in abundance along mature or meandering streams, they are located on the "outside" of a stream bend, known as a meander, opposite the slip-off slope on the inside of the bend.

 They are shaped much like a small cliff, and are formed by the erosion of soil as the stream collides with the river bank.

 As opposed to a point bar which is an area of deposition, a cut bank is an area of erosion.

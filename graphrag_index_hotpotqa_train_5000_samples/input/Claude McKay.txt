Claude McKay

Festus Claudius "Claude" McKay (September 15, 1889 – May 22, 1948) was a Jamaican writer and poet, who was a seminal figure in the Harlem Renaissance.

 He wrote four novels: "Home to Harlem" (1928), a best-seller that won the Harmon Gold Award for Literature, "Banjo" (1929), "Banana Bottom" (1933), and in 1941 a manuscript called "Amiable With Big Teeth: A Novel of the Love Affair Between the Communists and the Poor Black Sheep of Harlem" which remained unpublished until 2017.

 McKay also authored collections of poetry, a collection of short stories, "Gingertown" (1932), two autobiographical books, "A Long Way from Home" (1937) and "My Green Hills of Jamaica" (published posthumously), and a non-fiction, socio-historical treatise entitled "Harlem: Negro Metropolis" (1940).

 His 1922 poetry collection, "Harlem Shadows", was among the first books published during the Harlem Renaissance.

 His "Selected Poems" was published posthumously, in 1953.

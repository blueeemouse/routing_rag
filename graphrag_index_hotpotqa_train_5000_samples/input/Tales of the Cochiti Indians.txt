Tales of the Cochiti Indians

Tales of the Cochiti Indians is a 1931 work by Ruth Benedict.

 It collects the folk tales of the Cochiti Puebloan peoples in New Mexico.

 The book is considered an important work in the discipline of feminist anthropology.

 Following development of the "culture and personality" school of anthropology by her colleague Edward Sapir and influenced by Margaret Mead, Benedict sought psychological patterns in the stories she collected.

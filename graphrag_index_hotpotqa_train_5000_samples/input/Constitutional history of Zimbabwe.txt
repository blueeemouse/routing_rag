Constitutional history of Zimbabwe

The constitutional history of Zimbabwe starts with the arrival of white people to what was dubbed Southern Rhodesia in the 1890s.

 The country was initially run by an administrator appointed by the British South Africa Company.

 The prime ministerial role was first created in October 1923, when the country achieved responsible government, with Sir Charles Coghlan as its first Premier.

 The third Premier, George Mitchell, renamed the post Prime Minister in 1933.

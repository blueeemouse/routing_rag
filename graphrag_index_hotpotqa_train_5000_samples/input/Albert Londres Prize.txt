Albert Londres Prize

The Albert Londres Prize is the highest French journalism award, named in honor of journalist Albert Londres.

 Created in 1932, it was first awarded in 1933 and is considered the French equivalent of the Pulitzer Prize.

 However, unlike its American counterpart, it is only awarded to two laureates each year.

 The main prize goes to the year's "best reporter in the written press".

 Since 1985, a second prize has also been awarded to the "best audiovisual reporter".
